## Observations

1. **Merits: strong.** Every agent ranked the DBB ground strongest; GAO's per-ground rulings and remedy matched reality.
2. **Strategy: the agency diverged.** Simulated agency backed down early; the real one defended. Candidate explanations:
   - Assumed delay cost ($10K/day) drives corrective action — sensitivity test needed.
   - Claude cooperative bias (predicted in design §7).
   - Selection effect: published decisions only include agencies that defended, so this case is from the subset where the real agency judged its record defensible.
3. **Protester counsel was more disciplined than real counsel** (filed 3, dropped to 2; real filed 5). Credibility weight in scorecard may be too high, or real counsel optimizes differently (fees, client pressure).
4. **Belief gap by role:** agency's P(sustain DBB)=0.55 vs. protester counsel's 0.12→0.25. Information asymmetry visible: the party that knew the record was weak was most pessimistic about it.

## Leakage caveat

The agency-internal and protected-record facts were written from the published decision, which states some facts in GAO's framing (e.g., the 7th-ranked firm's main advantage). This favors correct rulings. Future packets should be built from the factual-background section only, written before reading GAO's analysis, ideally by a separate agent.
